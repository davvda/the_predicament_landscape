Post-complexity simplicity

Before one studies Zen, mountains are mountains and waters are waters; after a first glimpse into the truth of Zen, mountains are no longer mountains and waters are no longer waters; after enlightenment, mountains are once again mountains and waters once again waters.

- Dogen Zenji

Do you understand?

When we first approach something complex it often appears simple. We may wonder what the fuss is all about, what the problem is, why you'd need to study, train, practice, or change in order to master something so apparently simple. As we get nearer to the subject's core we see that it might not be as simple as we assumed and before we know it we are swallowed up by the mess, flailing confusedly, failing gloriously to make sense of anything at all. Either as suddenly as the overwhelming confusion hit you or as gradually as getting soaked in a delicate mist, you might, if you're lucky, once again arrive at something simple. This post-complexity simplicity, however, is nothing like the simplicity you started at. How does it differ? Keep going and you just might find out.

Concretely, be cautious and attentive to whether simple conclusions have been drawn before or after an ardent immersion in the complex, either in statements made by others or in your own thinking.

For the simplicity on this side of complexity, I wouldn't give you a fig. But for the simplicity on the other side of complexity, for that I would give you anything I have.

- Oliver Wendell Holmes

There is an additional interpretation of this overlay which is no less important. As children of modernity it is natural to think of developing countries and developed countries, a line of progress where humanity is destined to travel along the line as far as possible, away from diseases, inefficiencies, inconveniences, infant mortality, conflict, slavery, or whatever else riddles our dark history. You may cringe at the notion of "returning to simpler times", knowing that it sure was simpler but not necessarily good. I can not emphasise enough how important it is to recognise that a return to simplicity, a departure from our naïve progress narrative, the embracing of past ways of living as humans, is not in any way an actual return. It will be nothing like it was, it simply cannot be. If we decide or, the more likely outcome, are forced to live lives of simplicity in the coming years, decades, and centuries, it will be with the complexity of modernity behind us, the bad and the good. It will be simplicity in a world of complex pollution and climate change. It will be simplicity in a world which has mastered electricity and vaccines. Romanticise and idealise the past all you want, when we return to certain aspects of it the world will be a changed place, different mountains and different waters.
